WHAT stock AND exec ARE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Both columns come from ONE binary, the default-features (`vm` on) build, timed
on the SAME activation in the SAME process:

  stock ns   `Value::resolve_value` -- the tree walker, called DIRECTLY
  exec ns    `Program::execute` -- what a caller who names no features gets,
             which under the default `vm` feature is `cel::vm::cel_eval_loop`,
             the bytecode VM

So `ratio` = exec/stock is one activation through TWO evaluators under one
weather sample. It is not a difference between two builds, and it carries no
compile drift and no stale-binary risk. Each binary was verified by the
harness's own printed legend, not by filename.

CONTROL: in the `vm`-OFF build (`--no-default-features --features
regex,chrono,jit-cranelift`), where `exec` reaches the walker too, exec/stock
over all 54 rows is median 1.001x (range 0.942-1.041x). The
`Program::execute` wrapper therefore costs nothing measurable, and none of the
ratio below is the door rather than the VM.

*** THIS IS NOT A GATE ***
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Do not diff the ns columns run-to-run and call a difference a regression.
Absolute nanoseconds do NOT reproduce on this box -- the same measurement has
moved +/-10% between runs, and this run was taken under a load average of 17
rising to 41 on 18 cores. RATIOS reproduce: across the three rounds here, most
rows held within 2-3% and no row's sign or ranking moved.

The file exists for ONE purpose: so the NEXT re-measure can compute per-case
deltas. The 2026-08-21 re-measure could not. Its baseline (commit d69a40b) had
survived only as three summary numbers -- median 3.68x, min 1.57x
(nested_expression), max 8.14x (map_body/x) -- so of 33 rows exactly TWO could
be differenced, and every other per-case claim had to be withheld. Compare
ratios against this table; treat the ns columns as scale, not as a baseline.

HOW TO READ THIS FILE MECHANICALLY -- one trap, it has already fired
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A board file (`board_vm_r*.txt`) contains FOUR tables, and four separate lines
begin with `case ` (at lines 21, 90, 170 and 556 of a 583-line board). A
whitespace split over the whole file scrapes rows from all four and silently
mixes populations: it yields 62 rows instead of 54, and `nested_expression`
comes out as 275.3 / 157.8 -- which is `bind ns` / `resolve ns` from the
bind-split table, not stock/exec at all. That produces ratios below 1.0x and
a board on which the VM appears to WIN. It does not win anywhere.

Parse only the table whose header matches BOTH `stock ns` and `exec ns`, and
stop at the first blank line after its rows. Assert the row count.

RATIO ESTIMATOR -- the ns columns do NOT determine the ratio column
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recomputing exec/stock from the two published ns columns gives a DIFFERENT
number from the `ratio` column, and the difference is not rounding:

  case                        ratio (published)   exec_med/stock_med
  nested_expression                       1.65x                1.61x
  variable_access/resolver                6.86x                7.06x

Each round is a PAIRED observation: `stock` and `exec` are timed on the same
activation in the same process under one weather sample, and the pairing is
what makes the ratio meaningful. Taking the median of the per-round ratios
preserves it. Dividing two independently-taken medians discards it -- the two
medians need not even come from the same round, so the quotient describes no
run that was ever executed.

⚠ The wrong estimator can land ON this table's `lo` value and so LOOK like
agreement. For nested_expression, exec_med/stock_med = 1.61x, which is exactly
this row's `lo`. That is a coincidence, not a cross-check.

⚠ An assertion over rows and signs cannot catch this. `54 rows` and `every
ratio > 1.0` both PASS under either estimator: they check the population and
the direction, and neither can see which statistic produced the number. The
`lo`/`hi` columns are what make a wrong estimator visible at all -- a lone
ratio column would bury the disagreement in the third digit.

FOOTNOTE ON THE TWO variable_access ROWS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
They are the worst rows on this board (hashmap 6.79x, resolver 6.86x), having
displaced map_body/x at the top. CONVERGENCE.md records an earlier probe of
`variable_access/hashmap` under this same bench profile at 9.4 walker / 41.1 VM
= 4.4x (measured at cel-jit d60a60c -- a DIFFERENT commit, not the d69a40b
baseline). Against that: VM 41.1 -> 35.3 (-14%), walker 9.4 -> 5.0 (-47%).
The ratio worsened because THE WALKER GOT FASTER, not because the VM regressed.
A rising ratio is not by itself evidence of a regression in its numerator.
